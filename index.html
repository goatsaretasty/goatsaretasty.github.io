Case study

A shutter-to-retouch photo pipeline for warehouse operations

Trove Recommerce · Python, Tkinter, AppleScript, CaptureOne, Google Drive

The situation

Every physical item moving through Trove's warehouse needed photographs, metadata, and notes before it could be listed for resale. A first version of a capture app existed when I arrived — but it had aged, and I inherited it without its original author. Photographers were still juggling CaptureOne, camera hardware, item records, and file organization by hand, repeated for every single item at recommerce scale.

The approach

Rather than patch around the legacy version, I rebuilt it — owning the redesign and modernization end to end as the sole developer. The new app, written in Python and Tkinter, connected CaptureOne directly to the warehouse cameras through AppleScript. Single keystrokes captured the photo, its metadata, and item notes together, and every shot landed automatically in Google Drive — where a cron job carried files to the retouching service and delivered the finished images to their own Drive location. From shutter press to retouched photo, no one touched a file.

The result

Photographers kept their hands on the camera instead of the keyboard, metadata stopped going missing between systems, and the pipeline ran itself end to end. It's the pattern I keep coming back to: find the repeated manual work, and build the system that makes it disappear.
